# Browser Bestiary — Design

*Living game design document · updated 4 November 2026*  
[Play](https://hlywa.github.io/browser-bestiary/) · [Code](../sketch.js) · [Research and annotated bibliography](RESEARCH.md)

> An incomplete record of things found living here.

## 1. What this game is

A native-browser artificial-life observation and deduction game made in p5.js. Unfamiliar glyph-creatures move according to local rules. A player watches, intervenes, and completes a short, poetic natural-history entry by inferring the behavior. Correct inferences enter a persistent bestiary. The world is a place to return to, not merely a puzzle screen.

**Experience target:** The pleasure of gradually recognizing an alien logic, including the unsettling possibility that our first sympathetic explanation was wrong. Play should make the player look again.

**Desired emotional register:** Quiet, strange, faintly mournful, curious, funny in its taxonomic specificity. Sadness should grow from the modeled constraints of a creature's existence, not from arbitrary tragic lore. The poems should be beautiful *and* answerable to observed behavior.

**Broader ambition, not this prototype's promise:** A “thinking Proteus” with unfamiliar life and interpretations as consequential as deductions in *Return of the Obra Dinn* or *The Case of the Golden Idol*. The browser sketch is an intentionally smaller native-web experiment in that direction.

### Boundaries
- A first-order playable experience can be generated by **two linked design spaces**: a simulation of creature behavior and a structured vocabulary of hypotheses about it.
- Artificial organisms need not imitate real animals. But the program must specify what they sense, require, remember, and do.
- Avoid a beautiful animation pretending to be ALife; avoid turning discovery into an answer-sheet quiz.
- Treat the player's interpretive vocabulary as part of the designed environment. The poem's choices guide attention as much as they record understanding.
- Preserve surprise: do not name a creature's internal motivations in neutral observation text before the player can discover them.

## 3. Core loop and interaction grammar

**Arrival → encounter → observation → provisional hypothesis → intervention (optional) → revision → poem → recorded entry → next encounter.**

1. **Arrive.** A black clearing and small unexplained creatures. The initial title has an atmospheric function; while playing, a modest title remains for identity in screenshots.
2. **Attend.** Watch paths, speeds, stops, proximity and concentration. Time must matter. Let an agent surprise you before the interface explains it.
3. **Hypothesize.** Open the lower-third book. Each line is a causal claim framed as a small field poem. Choices should be mutually discriminable, not merely differently pretty.
4. **Test.** Move or remove environmental affordances, scatter a group, or rerun the observation. Do **not** force a button press just to satisfy submission. Experimentation should be useful, not an arbitrary gate.
5. **Revise.** A wrong answer highlights the unsupported line; the book and field remain accessible without resetting selections.
6. **Record.** Correct lines form a stable bestiary poem; local browser storage remembers entries with a tiny species drawing, optional initial field note, optional prediction, and optional revised observation.
7. **Next encounter.** The dedicated main-field toolbar button appears on capture. Closing the book only closes the book. **Return** remains a future design idea: time away, changing conditions and intermittent sightings, without accessing other browser-tab contents.

### What counts as skill?
A successful player can make an explanation that survives a change in conditions, not merely memorize the three dropdowns. For a later playtest, ask for a prediction **before** intervening, then show the consequences. Distinguish **observation** (they collect here), **prediction** (moving the zone will change the distribution), and **mechanism** (they slow down locally without seeking each other).

### Problems exposed by first informal playtest
From conversation and field observation, not a controlled study:
- Directional creature silhouettes were immediately legible, evoking the early cellular phase of *Spore*. **Keep** head/tail and travel orientation.
- The tester first read “the pale hollow” as detached text, not a place. The boundary and environmental role need a more legible but still restrained presentation.
- The player spontaneously hypothesized a **timer alternating solitude and sociability**, a motivation that **does not exist in the current code**. Important evidence of how observers invent internal causes.
- The poem choices “sculpted” what the tester looked for. This is a key design discovery worth testing later.
- The opening book concealed the experiment controls; revisions put controls persistently at the top and slimmed the book.
- No wrong-line feedback and no readable answer key made uncertainty indistinguishable from a bug; now line-level feedback and Shift+D debug exist.
- The original mandatory intervention gate prevented capturing even with a correct solution. **Removed**: a correct poem is sufficient.
- The tester questioned whether creatures were “gathering” at all: they may be merely lingering. **Language corrected** from “They gather” to “They are found.”
- The test included irritation with switching between book and clearing. Keep reducing friction; do not interpret interface failure as intellectual difficulty.

### Interface design decisions
- Monochrome, spare line-art glyph creatures; serif editorial type and small italic annotations. No default gray/beige paper panel.
- **Three screen states:** arrival, active observation, book open. The title is big only on arrival. Active HUD remains unobtrusive.
- **World first:** opening the journal must not render the organisms impossible to watch.
- Book is a **thin, scrollable lower panel**, not a full-screen form. Two columns on desktop, single column on narrow screens.
- The three general experiments remain in the persistent top toolbar. A fourth control, **silence / restore the howl**, is shown only in the Howler mixed-species study. Minimize stacked explanations; contextual help could be revealed on demand.
- Experiment and journal states must not accidentally reset each other. Closing the book **no longer** advances the study. A separate next-encounter control in the observation toolbar explicitly advances.
- The “field reading” (occupancy of hollow / mean nearest-neighbor distance) is implemented but currently suppressed by some layout rules. Decide whether it should be an optional field instrument, not a permanent exposed scoreboard.
- Debug Shift+D is for developers, not for the shipped game. A visible test-mode badge is preferable if testing with others.
- Use consistent keyboard navigation, focus states, readable hit targets, and test at actual laptop dimensions.
- Avoid cosmetic creature variety masking the absence of behavioral variety.

## 4. The present simulation: exactly what the code models

**World:** 1000×560 logical canvas. Favorable circular region starts at (620,270) with radius 155.  
**Agents:** 24. Movement now advances with a fixed **50 ms simulation step** (20 updates/second), independent of drawing frame rate; long inactive-tab catch-up is capped. Directions and positions use seeded pseudo-random initial values.  
**Boundaries:** agents reflect from edges.  
**Mechanism A: environmental dwell time (currently study 1, and later repeated studies).** A creature randomly turns (roughly ±0.5 radians per update). It moves about 2.2 pixels outside the circle, but only 0.23 pixels inside it. It does **not** sense, locate, or aim at the distant hollow; clustering can arise from dwelling longer in a place that is encountered by chance.  
**Mechanism B: social attraction (currently study 2 and 5, before cycling).** A creature averages positions of neighbors within a radius of 520 pixels, turns toward that average with bounded steering and noise, and moves about 1.1–1.7 pixels/update. It does **not** sense the hollow. With no short-range repulsion, it may collapse into an unrealistic knot.

**Controls:** “move the pale hollow” toggles its center (not available in the Afterling study, where there is no pale hollow); “disturb the gathering” scatters agents; “repeat observation” randomizes starts while retaining the active mechanism. In study 4, “silence / restore the howl” toggles visible calls without changing pursuit. The system now has one simple predator interaction and a Howler hunger variable, but still no growth, genetic inheritance, reproduction, chemical traces or population evolution.

**Important limitation:** A faint circle denotes favorable habitat for mechanism A, but means **nothing** to mechanism B. This creates a useful hypothesis test, but also risks a misleading visual prior. The code's two rules are not yet matched for visually similar clustering. Before treating it as a fair causal inference task, measure both and tune the comparison.

### Pilot diagnostic retained from DESIGN_NOTES.md (3 Nov 2026)
A separate *headless Python approximation*, not a validated reproduction of the browser build, ran 8 seeds, 24 agents, 1200 updates, moving the region after step 600. Fraction inside the region:

| Rule | Step 600 | Step 750 | Step 1200 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Habitat dwell time | 0.422 | 0.188 | 0.307 |
| Social attraction | 0.797 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Approximate mean nearest-neighbor spacing at step 600: habitat 74.3 px; social 5.7 px. **Do not infer equivalence or statistical generality.** This was a small engineering diagnostic and the difference itself signals a design issue. ## 5. Creature grammar (proposed design space)

Create coherent organisms from **perception × need × response × memory × social relation × environmental feedback × timescale**, rather than five unrelated animations.

| Dimension | Questions to specify | Possible levers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sensors | What local condition is detectable? What remains inaccessible? | Patch quality, contact, sound/stimulus, neighbor radius, trace concentration |
| Needs | What condition sustains the organism? | Energy budget, rest time, resource, shelter, social cue |
| Actions | How does a perception alter movement or the environment? | Linger, flee, follow, mark, feed, signal, imitate |
| Memory | Can past encounters matter after the stimulus leaves? | None, short timer, retained internal state, environmental trace |
| Relations | Does interaction involve others directly or indirectly? | No sensing, attraction/repulsion, competition, mutualism, exploitation |
| Time | Do effects accumulate, decay or reproduce? | Signal decay, fatigue, birth/death, resource regeneration |
| Observable clue | What can an observer see without omniscience? | Speed, direction, spacing, clustering, delays, disappearance |
| Disambiguating test | What intervention distinguishes rival explanations? | Move habitat, scatter organisms, interrupt signal, remove a species |
| Poem | What three-to-five **testable** statements can be expressed beautifully? | Place, behavior, relation, environmental change, consequence |

### Working roster: five genuinely different encounters, not yet implemented

**01. Edgling · environmental dwell time · 3 lines**
- **Perceives:** immediate habitat quality; not other Edglings, not a remote destination.
- **Need (planned):** sufficient time in favorable conditions to maintain energy; current code models only the change in speed.
- **Rule:** slows after encountering favorable terrain; wandering produces aggregation without social navigation.
- **False interpretation:** affection, companionship, or intentional rendezvous.
- **Discriminating test:** scatter them or relocate the hollow; look for delayed occupancy without deliberate pursuit.
- **Potential lines:** where it remains; what changes after the habitat moves; what it cannot perceive.
- **Model precedents:** [NetLogo Habitat Preference Behavior](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/HabitatPreferenceBehavior); [Braitenberg, *Vehicles*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262521123/vehicles/). The NetLogo model uses a comparable dwell-time principle but is not identical code.
- **Status:** current first encounter is a placeholder for this intended fuller organism.

**02. Hushling · directional response to a limited signal · 3 lines**
- **Perceives:** one signal and its local strength, not a human-defined “threat.”
- **Need:** avoid damaging stimulation / maintain tolerable signal level, if this is explicitly modeled.
- **Rule concept:** approach, retreat or freeze based on signal threshold and hysteresis.
- **False interpretation:** love, fear, or obedience toward another creature.
- **Player test:** move signal source; interrupt stimulus; compare orientation after signal loss.
- **Potential lines:** what it hears or feels; how it turns; what it mistakes as absent.
- **Model precedents:** Braitenberg-style sensor-motor agents, [NetLogo Moths](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/Moths).
- **Visual design (implemented 4 November 2026):** Hushlings now use a miniature bird-like outline, instead of the previous upright oval that resembled an Edgling. An angular wedge body, raised round head, single pointed beak, folded wing tick, short tail flick and two fine legs give the silhouette a distinctive directional profile. Keep the glyph spare, readable at small size and consistent with the monochrome line-art system. Its saved bestiary portrait shares this design, including for existing Hushling entries when the app renders them.
- **Status:** **visual redesign implemented; behavior remains provisional.** Study 002 still uses the existing neighbor-attraction rule, not a full independent sensorimotor ecology. Do not infer bird-like behavior from its new depiction.

**03. Afterling · stigmergy and fading traces · 4 lines**
- **Perceives:** local environmental trace concentration, not the identity of the leaving organism.
- **Need:** access to a resource, home site, or viable route.
- **Rule concept:** agents deposit, detect and reinforce a decaying mark; routes emerge without explicit map sharing.
- **False interpretation:** inherited memory, a deliberate promise, faithful pursuit of an absent companion.
- **Player test:** interrupt the path, change evaporation, remove trace source and observe persistence.
- **Potential lines:** what it follows; who left it; when the trace fades; what survives disappearance.
- **Model precedent:** [NetLogo Ants](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/Ants).
- **Status (4 November 2026):** **First playable trace mechanism implemented.** The Afterling uses a sparse crescent/needle glyph with forked feelers, rather than another oval. Study 3 renders a field of faint, branching/decaying trace marks instead of the pale hollow. A 50×28 grid stores environmental intensity. Organisms compare locally sensed marks ahead and diagonally forward, steer toward stronger traces, and deposit new marks as they move. Trace intensity decays by ×0.994 per 50 ms simulation step. Two crooked old routes initialize the experiment, and participants can **erase all traces** to observe new paths forming. The original habitat button is hidden for this study; “disturb the gathering” becomes “scatter the afterlings.” This is an intentional simplified stigmergy model, not a validated analogue of animal pheromone trails, route choice or social memory. The Afterling now has its own three-claim field poem rather than repeating the habitat/social answer key; the four-line version remains a future writing/design proposal.

**04. Howler · predation, signaling and prey responses · proposed 4 lines**
- **Silhouette (implemented):** a tiny impressionistic wolf, matching the other creatures at roughly 20–30 pixels. Thin white outlines on black; a single oval body; one pointed ear; raised short snout; small open mouth with **one tooth**; a dot eye; stick legs; a line tail; two short howl marks. No fur rendering, hatching, complex anatomy or shading. The approved visual study is a *gesture*, not a detailed wolf illustration.
- **Visual-reference sequence:** early cat/wolf sketches led to a detailed white-line howling wolf, which was repeatedly simplified against the existing Edgling-like glyph reference. Final accepted direction is a naive line sketch with a few strokes; the in-game p5 drawing is a programmatic approximation. The reference was supplied in the project conversation on 4 November 2026.
- **Ecological role (proposed):** one of the first dangerous organisms, potentially a predator of Edglings. Its howl may be causal in hunting, incidental to hunger, territorial, or misinterpreted by the observer. **Do not assume its purpose before specifying and testing a rule.**
- **Perception (to specify):** perhaps prey proximity, motion or a local vibration cue; limit what it can actually sense.
- **Internal state (to specify):** hunger/energy, detection threshold and howl timing, if the selected hypothesis requires them.
- **Actions (to specify):** investigate, call, approach or bite under explicit constraints; prey might freeze, scatter or lose orientation. Pick one mechanism initially, rather than adding all at once.
- **False interpretation:** “It warns others,” “the prey fears its voice,” or “it is calling a companion.” Alternate hypotheses must generate distinguishable outcomes.
- **Player experiment (candidate):** interrupt or relocate the signal; separate predator and prey; change access to the prey's favorable zone. Determine whether the response is to sound, presence or movement.
- **Poetic task:** describe a signal, what happens to nearby bodies, a predation or resource relation, and the limits of what the observer can infer about its intention.
- **Research question:** which observation justifies describing an interaction as predation, rather than apparent following or mutual attendance?
- **Model precedents:** [NetLogo Wolf Sheep Predation](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/WolfSheepPredation) for actual survival dynamics; Braitenberg-style sensory response for signal-based behavior. These are separate components, not yet an integrated Howler model.
- **Status (4 November, second pass):** The Howler encounter now runs a **small mixed predator-prey demonstration**, not the shared social/habitat rule. Two Howlers hunt 22 Edglings; hunger triggers pursuit, nearby prey detect predators and move away, captures temporarily remove prey, and the visible howl can be silenced without changing predator/prey response. **Still absent:** actual sound synthesis, sensory evolution, genuine energy budgets for prey, birth/death population modeling, multiple predator strategies, experimental validation or a full player study. Its three-line poem now tests separate claims, but the choice text may itself reveal mechanisms.

**Mourner (retired working slot, preserved as a future relationship concept):** The earlier fourth creature was a proposed ecological dependency, possibly mutualism, competition or predation. It is now replaced by the Howler in the first-five roster. Its central idea remains available as an *encounter* in which a species seems to mourn the disappearance of another, although it may simply depend on that other organism's effects on the environment. Do not call this implemented.

**05. Manyone · leaderless collective organization · 5 lines**
- **Perceives:** neighbor and/or local chemical cues rather than global collective shape.
- **Need:** favorable conditions or coordination emerging from local rules.
- **Rule concept:** local attraction, repulsion and possible short-lived coupling produce a group form. Alternative: aggregation and separation of a slime-like distributed organism.
- **False interpretation:** a leader, a single mind, a shared plan.
- **Player test:** separate clusters, block signaling, change sensing range, remove alleged “leader.”
- **Potential lines:** what the units see; the group they form; what holds the boundary; what happens after division; whether anyone commands the whole.
- **Model precedents:** [NetLogo Flocking](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/Flocking), [NetLogo Slime](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/Slime).
- **Status:** proposed; actual social attraction code is not a full flocking model.

### Combinations after introductory encounters
A richer design space comes when **known creatures interact** in one world. Candidate examples, not implemented: an Edgling's favorable patch changes the signal field the Hushling can perceive; an Afterling's mark changes where another creature travels; a Howler's presence or signal changes Edgling survival or movement; a Manyone swarm blocks or amplifies a cue. Test whether players transfer knowledge acquired in separate encounters to a novel combination.

## 6. Field poetry, diction, and inference

**Form:** each line finishes a sentence in a natural-history poem. Initial difficulty: two 3-line entries, then one 4-line and one 5-line entry was discussed; the current five-species progression implies the final exact distribution is still open. Do not create filler to satisfy a line count.

- Every mechanically “correct” line makes a defensible claim about the **code and observable outcome**.
- Sadness and estrangement come from the difference between the observer's projections and what the simulated creature can actually know.
- A line can be metaphorical, but distinguish metaphor from a demonstrated computational fact.
- Prefer precise verbs: linger vs gather; turn vs pursue; follow a trace vs remember a friend; aggregate vs choose to cooperate.
- Each wrong alternative should express a plausible rival mechanism, not an obvious joke or nonsense.
- Don't reveal the correct mechanism through the name, species story, or visible help text.
- The poem can direct attention to a test before the player knows its result.

**Edgling provisional writing**
> Where the ground is kind, it lingers.  
> It has no eyes for those beside it.  
> They spend their little lives together, never knowing.

The last line is a poetic conjecture about inner life. Its exact subjectivity is **not** supported by the sensor rules. Keep that tension visible in [research](RESEARCH.md).

**Current in-game choices:** three stems “They are found…”, “When the world is changed…”, “What binds them is…”. Each stem offers habitat/social/random answers. All three currently must match the same hidden mechanism; this is a first prototype of inference composition, **not** yet a sophisticated independent fact model. Dev mode shows the exact phrases.

**References by function:** Zhu Yingchun's creature bestiary for speculative taxonomic miniature; Werner Herzog's nature language for dreadful wonder; [goodbye.monster](https://goodbye.monster/) for web-native melancholy and shifting interface; [Proteus](https://www.visitproteus.com/) for dwelling in atmosphere; [The Case of the Golden Idol](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1677770/) and *Return of the Obra Dinn* for deduction through reconstructed causal statements. These are influences, not claims of shared technical mechanisms.

## 7. Progression and cadence

- **Now:** one testable environment with two hidden rules. Record one poem and proceed. Different name/glyph does not yet mean new species.
- **First complete vertical slice:** one Edgling with known perceptual constraints, a legible pattern, at least one useful intervention, three justified answers, saved field poem, and reliable next encounter.
- **Five-creature edition:** each creature introduces an independent kind of sensing/interaction and a distinct inference challenge. Complexity can grow through relations, not just number of lines.
- **Later:** mixed ecologies, changing landscapes, return visits, short-lived sightings, possible artistic watercolor variants. Do not add web-history surveillance; ordinary p5.js cannot inspect arbitrary browser tab contents.
- **Even later, if research supports it:** adaptive behavior, inheritance, genetic algorithms, evolving populations. These require explicit variation/selection/inheritance or within-lifetime learning, not a label.

## 8. Playtest and revision notebook (folded into design)

### First formative session — November 2026, conversational transcript
**Observed, not measured:** Tester liked orientation of the glyphs and found the poem choices suggestive. Tester misunderstood the pale hollow, formed unsupported social/timer theories, disliked blocked controls and lack of feedback, and requested a developer answer key. The interaction showed that **poem text can cue observation**. It does not show that the game improved understanding or that the puzzle is calibrated. No participant metrics collected.

**Changes implemented afterward:** click-away and closeable book, persistent field controls, line feedback, debug panel with answer texts, removal of intervention gate, start screen with small persistent title, lower book height. Subsequent screenshots confirmed meaningful portions of the revised UI but also exposed spacing and usability limitations.

### Open design questions
- How long must a first-time visitor watch before an interesting pattern is perceptible? In real elapsed seconds, not merely simulation ticks?
- Should players be able to pause, accelerate, or annotate time? Does time control alter their inference strategies?
- Is the hollow clearly a zone but not too strongly a “correct answer” signpost?
- Does moving the hollow produce a reliable enough result within a session?
- Are spatial clusters matched enough across mechanisms to require intervention, or is the answer visible immediately?
- Should wrong-line feedback be withheld in the final artistic version but retained in test mode?
- Does a poem feel authored if all the choices are predefined? Could the bestiary later support an optional freeform field note distinct from validated inferences?
- Should events change when the tab loses or regains focus, and how can that be meaningful rather than manipulative?
- How many saved entries are interesting before the player perceives repetitions?
- How does a player recognize a new creature from its behavior, not just from a new silhouette?

### Next implementation gates
1. **Behavioral validation:** match and document both agents' cluster-size distributions, neighbor distances, occupancy and response to interventions over repeated runs.
2. **Sensor ecology:** specify individual perceptual inputs and why each would create each observable behavior; refactor agent logic into creature-specific models rather than modulo-based cosmetics.
3. **Inference redesign:** line answers map to independently observed facts/causes, not three synonyms for a hidden code switch.
4. **Field-study protocol:** player prediction → deliberate test → observed consequence → revised explanation. Measure success without forcing arbitrary interaction.
5. **Five-organism vertical roster:** move to next only when the current organism yields a meaningful and legible discovery.

### Browser Bestiary v0.2 integration — 4 November 2026

**Implementation scope (code committed; logic-tested in a simulated DOM, but live browser visuals and performance still need manual validation):**

- **Field-level progression:** a “next encounter” button belongs to the top observation toolbar, appears only after a poem has been recorded, and is the sole way to advance. Closing the book preserves the current field.
- **Bestiary portraits:** each recorded entry has a small black-and-white SVG glyph beside its poem. The Edgling sprite matches the original oval, eye, and simple limbs. Older saved entries are recognized from their creature name when no `speciesId` exists.
- **Player interpretation history:** the guessing page now has optional **first impression**, **prediction**, and **after observing** fields. All three are saved separately with the poem, not used as scoring gates. A later research design must account for notes written after the fact; the current interface does not timestamp each individual hypothesis.
- **Howler ecology:** encounter 4 now contains **22 Edglings and 2 Howlers**. Edglings locally slow within the pale hollow (0.23 vs. 2.2 px/update), but move away from predators within 100 px. Howlers accrue hunger (+0.28 per update), search for Edglings within 280 px, and once sufficiently hungry (14) steer toward them, moving at 3 px/update and capturing within 15 px. Captured Edglings disappear for 240 simulation updates, then respawn at a random location. Howlers reset hunger on capture. This is a deliberately bounded prototype, **not** biological reproduction.
- **Call:** when hungry, Howlers periodically emit a visual pulse (at a 150-tick phase, for 24 updates), with sound marks and an expanding ring. A field control silences/restores that **visual call**. It currently has **no causal influence** on Edgling flight or Howler hunting: proximity determines prey response, while hunger and visibility govern pursuit. It is not yet an actual audio signal.
- **Howler poem:** independent answer values about what is sought, how Edglings react to approach, and whether the cry drives that response. Existing encounters still use a two-category rule key.
- **Developer tools:** Shift+D shows the per-line answer phrases and the Howler's captured prey counter. The entry list loads existing localStorage notes on startup.

**Engineering caution:** The browser simulation was updated to fixed 50 ms steps after the initial v0.2 pass. This reduces rendering-rate dependence, but the full ecology has **not yet** been validated against seeds or real browser performance. The pre-existing headless Python comparison is **not** validation of the mixed system. Run seeded browser trials and actual player observations before concluding that its outcomes are legible, reliable or surprising. The howl's visibility should not be mistaken for acoustic input.

**Next action:** manually inspect the new fourth encounter in an actual browser at desktop and mobile widths; then verify capture frequency across multiple seeds and determine whether the silence intervention is genuinely informative or just exposes a trivial non-causal visual cue. Then give Hushling/Afterling/Manyone their own rules.

### Afterling redesign: field study 003 — 4 November 2026

**Problem observed in actual screenshot:** Study 3 felt like a cosmetic Edgling variant: near-identical oval-bodied silhouettes, the same alternating `habitat` movement, and the same single pale circle. This was a failure of behavioral diversity, environmental variety, and inference, not merely an art problem.

**Changes in code:**
- **A new morphology:** a small open crescent/needle with forked antennae and two legs, still thin white linework on black. Its bestiary portrait uses the same glyph language.
- **A different scene:** remove the pale circular hollow altogether and draw very faint decaying trace marks over the clearing. The initially visible paths bend and fork; later traces can develop based on movement.
- **An independent simulated behavior:** a 50×28 environmental trace field. Each Afterling samples trace strength in three directions roughly 30 px ahead, gently turns toward the stronger cue, otherwise wanders, and deposits trace as it moves. Traces decay and can disappear unless reinforced. This is stigmergy via a shared mutable environment, not individual memory or explicit following of another creature.
- **A different experiment:** the top toolbar substitutes **erase the traces** for **move the pale hollow**. The other movement intervention becomes **scatter the afterlings**; repeat observation can restore starting paths.
- **A distinct three-line field poem:** one line on following marks, one on the environment holding traces, and one on what happens when those traces vanish. The existing three-line UI is preserved. An intended four-line poetic version remains future work.

**Important model limitations:** Pre-existing traces are deliberately preseeded rather than generated by a prior run of agents. The trace grid and follow strength require tuning and seed-based tests; following a path while adding to it does not yet establish stable collective route formation. This version is a playable model proposal, not scientific validation. The same change may make the intended answer obvious because the traces are visible. Observe players before deciding whether to reveal traces fully or only after intervention.

### Howler visual integration — 4 November 2026

**Decision:** Add the Howler to the five-creature roster, replacing the Mourner as a named species. Use the extremely sparse impressionistic line language of the existing p5 creatures, not the detailed wolf concept art.

**Implementation:** `drawAgent()` has a small dedicated Howler branch; the encounter names now cycle Edglings → Hushlings → Afterlings → Howlers → Manyones. The Howler is recognizable through one pointed ear, raised toothed muzzle, sparse feet and tail. Two small howl strokes appear only during its visual call. It is deliberately a silhouette, not an elaborate drawing.

**Historical note:** The first Howler commit was a visual-only pass. The later v0.2 pass added a preliminary mixed-species predation mechanism and separate poem choices, as described above; do not mistake this initial implementation for a completed validated ecology.

### Replay and reset for developer testing — 4 November 2026

**Observed problem and root cause:** On startup `loadEntries()` used `max(savedStudyNumber) + 1`, so refreshing after studies 001 and 002 unconditionally opened study 003. The earlier “restart” control appeared **below** all the saved entries and was destructive. It did not affect ordinary refresh behavior unless the user explicitly reached and confirmed the wipe.

**Correction:** Keep the saved bestiary in `browser-bestiary-field-poems-v1` and the independently selected current study in `browser-bestiary-current-study-v1`. The stored study cursor takes precedence on load, while previous saves without a cursor still resume at the study after the last recorded entry (backward compatibility).

**Two clearly separated actions in different places:**
- **↺ restart at 001:** a small, **always-visible main-field toolbar button**, available even when the book is closed. Immediately returns to the Edglings, saves the study cursor, and **preserves all recorded poems**. Refreshing keeps the replay position; “next encounter” increments and persists it.
- **Erase saved entries:** remains in the recorded-entries page of the bestiary, requires confirmation, deletes only the game's entries and cursor, and returns to study 001. This is irreversible for notes stored only in the browser.

**Regression check:** with two saved entries and no cursor: initial load → 003; replay → 001 with two entries intact; refresh → 001; advancement → 002; refresh → 002; erase → 001 with zero entries; refresh → 001. This check tests the JavaScript storage/transition logic using a simulated environment. Visual and user-interaction validation in a live browser remains necessary.

The separation is important for research: replaying an experiment need not destroy its previous field records. Replaying and submitting again can yield multiple field entries with the same study number, intentionally preserving repeat observations rather than overwriting them.
